# Resume template & typography research

Evidence base for the template rebuild. Every claim is cited to a published source:
university career-centre guides, recruiter/employer guidance, ATS vendor documentation,
and print typography references. Where sources conflict, §2 records the conflict and the
product resolution. Numbers in §3–§4 are the recommended canonical values derived from
this evidence.

Sources were fetched and verified 2026-10-24. Where a source was sought but not
retrievable, that is stated in §9.

---

## 1. What the credible sources actually agree on

### 1.1 Body size: 10–12 pt, with 10 pt as the practical floor

- "In print, the optimal point size for body text is 10–12 point." ([Butterick, Practical Typography — Point size](https://practicaltypography.com/point-size.html))
- "Use a conservative font no smaller than 10pt and leave at least half inch margins on all sides." ([MIT CAPD — Resumes](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resumes/))
- "Size 10 – 12 size font are recommended. Margins no smaller than 0.5”." ([MIT CAPD Career Toolkit, 2024, p. 11](https://cdn.uconnectlabs.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/123/2025/01/MIT-CAPDs-Career-Toolkit-Crafting-an-effective-resume.pdf))
- "11-12 point font … 0.5 - 1 inch margins" for resumes; "11-12 point font, 1-inch margins" for CVs. ([Georgia Tech Career Center — Resume Career Guide, 2024, p. 12](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf))
- "Try not to go smaller than 11 pt. font." ([Georgia Tech GT 1000 textbook — Resume Appearance and Organization](https://sites.gatech.edu/gt1000-textbook/career-development/resumes-linkedin-and-more/resume-appearance-and-organization/))
- "Use a sans serif font such as Arial, keep it to size 11 or above." ([Imperial College London — Academic CVs tip-sheet](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/staff-development/public/postdocs/tipsheets/Academic-CVs-v2019.pdf))
- "Keep your font size between 10 and 12 points." ([Indeed Career Guide — How Long Should a Resume Be?](https://au.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/how-long-should-a-resume-be))

Convergence: **10 pt absolute floor, 11 pt preferred floor, 12 pt ceiling for body.**
The same point size renders at different apparent sizes across families, so the final
value must be adjusted per family ([Butterick — Point size](https://practicaltypography.com/point-size.html)).

### 1.2 Margins: 0.5 in is the floor, 1 in is the classical norm

- "leave at least half inch margins on all sides" ([MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resumes/)); "Margins no smaller than 0.5”" ([MIT CAPD Toolkit](https://cdn.uconnectlabs.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/123/2025/01/MIT-CAPDs-Career-Toolkit-Crafting-an-effective-resume.pdf)).
- "Have margins between .5 - 1”" ([GT Career Center Guide, p. 10](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf)); "Keep left and right margins no smaller than one-half inch, and ideally one full inch." ([GT 1000](https://sites.gatech.edu/gt1000-textbook/career-development/resumes-linkedin-and-more/resume-appearance-and-organization/))
- "stick to standard margins (1 inch/2.54 cm all the way round)" ([Imperial College](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/staff-development/public/postdocs/tipsheets/Academic-CVs-v2019.pdf))

Convergence: **0.5 in (36 pt) never; 0.5–1 in for industry resumes; 1 in for academic CVs.**

### 1.3 Line height and density

- "For most text, the optimal line spacing is between 120% and 145% of the point size."
  "Line spacing affects the length of a document more than point size. If you need to fit a
  document onto a certain number of pages, try adjusting the line spacing first."
  ([Butterick — Line spacing](https://practicaltypography.com/line-spacing.html))
- "As you reduce point size, also reduce line spacing and line length." ([Butterick — Point size](https://practicaltypography.com/point-size.html))
- Dense resumes are explicitly permitted to be tight: "Recruiters would much rather read two
  well-organised pages full of extremely relevant and helpful information than one page that's
  difficult to read and crammed with information at a small font point." ([Indeed](https://au.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/how-long-should-a-resume-be))

Convergence: the **1.2–1.45 band**, with the tight end (≈1.2–1.3) appropriate for resume
density; readability, not page count, is the constraint.

### 1.4 Whitespace is structural, not decorative

- "Make it easy to read and follow, balancing white space." ([Harvard FAS MCS — Guide to Creating a Strong Resume](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/))
- "Separate sections with white space for clear delineation of content categories." ([GT 1000](https://sites.gatech.edu/gt1000-textbook/career-development/resumes-linkedin-and-more/resume-appearance-and-organization/))
- "a visually appealing resume with a balance of text and open space draws a reader in, while a
  cluttered, crowded resume with too many styling elements does nothing to invite a careful read." ([GT 1000](https://sites.gatech.edu/gt1000-textbook/career-development/resumes-linkedin-and-more/resume-appearance-and-organization/))
- "Maintain uniformity of length and construction of bullets." ([GT 1000](https://sites.gatech.edu/gt1000-textbook/career-development/resumes-linkedin-and-more/resume-appearance-and-organization/))

### 1.5 Emphasis is done with weight and case, sparingly

- "Use bold text sparingly to highlight key information or section headings." ([MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resumes/))
- "Bold font works well for headings and emphasising job titles, but capitals, italics, and
  underlining can clutter the page and are often best avoided." ([Imperial College](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/staff-development/public/postdocs/tipsheets/Academic-CVs-v2019.pdf))
- "Use consistent spacing, underlining, italics, bold, and capitalization for emphasis" — i.e.
  pick a small set of devices and apply them consistently. ([Harvard FAS MCS](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/))
- "Bold only those items that you wish to draw attention to." ([University of Toronto — Resume & Cover Letter Toolkit, 2024, p. 3](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf))
- Emphasis by size should be minimal: "If your body text is set at 11 point, no need to jump to
  14 point for emphasis. Start with a smaller increase—say, half a point." And heading size
  inflation is a web habit, not typography: "There is no typographic universe in which you need
  to double the point size to achieve emphasis." ([Butterick — Point size](https://practicaltypography.com/point-size.html))

### 1.6 ATS parsing: single column, plain text, standard headings, no embedded objects

From **Greenhouse's own support documentation** ("Unsuccessful resume parse"), the named
causes of a failed parse are ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)):

- a file larger than 2.5 MB;
- "A resume with spaces between the letters" — letter-spaced heading words;
- "Resumes that include graphics, photos, or word art";
- resumes uploaded as an image rather than a document;
- "Complex resumes with tables, headers, and footers";
- "Resumes with the name and contact information in the header, footer, or text box";
- "Resumes that have a columned layout";
- "Resumes without clear sections and differing formats throughout each section";
- abbreviated job titles ("Sr. Account Exec" instead of "Senior Account Executive") and
  company names without identifying words (Inc., Co., LTD, LLC).

From **Workable** (an ATS vendor), what parsers need ([Workable — What is resume parsing?](https://resources.workable.com/stories-and-insights/how-ATS-reads-resumes)):

- "use standard formatting, avoid fancy embellishments, and follow typical section titles";
- "Try to avoid using headers and footers as they often get interspersed with the main body of text";
- "Use one standard font throughout the CV";
- "Don't use tables and columns as the ordering of sentences may not be what you expect";
- "Don't use WordArt" and "Don't fiddle with character spacing" — "Large headings, unusual
  character spacing and font choice can result in spaces being created or lost";
- "Use typical names for section titles like 'Education', 'Work Experience', 'Personal Details'";
- "Stick to a chronological resume format, not a functional one";
- "Use a date format appropriate to the country you are applying in and make sure you include
  dates in full … to make them easily identifiable";
- "Only use common and well known abbreviations" and "Use typical names for job titles".

How multi-column layouts fail: "A parser generally reads a page as a single stream of text, left
to right, top to bottom. A two-column layout gets read one row at a time across both columns …
the result is a garbled record even though the file opened perfectly on your own screen." The
same source notes that neither Workday nor Greenhouse specifies a font, margin or export
setting that "beats" a parser; Workday states its parsing results vary by format and word order
([Olive — Test Whether Your Resume Format Breaks a Parser](https://olive.is/answers/resume-file-format-parser-breaks/),
citing [Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)
and [Workday docs](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/human-capital-management/recruiting/candidates/set-up-prospects-and-candidates/hdc1552497830785.html)).

Practical consequence: a failed parse is not a rejection — "you'll need to manually input the
candidate's details into the fields" ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse))
— but a clean file removes a whole failure class.

### 1.7 Length norms

- "Stick to one page, unless you have extensive experience or an advanced degree." ([MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resumes/)); "Typically keep your resume to one page, two pages max (CVs are longer)." ([MIT CAPD Toolkit](https://cdn.uconnectlabs.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/123/2025/01/MIT-CAPDs-Career-Toolkit-Crafting-an-effective-resume.pdf))
- "A one-page resume is fairly standard, especially for students, new graduates and professionals
  with one to 10 years of experience." Multi-page is acceptable when the content earns it; "err
  on the side of one page, but not at the expense of readability." ([Indeed](https://au.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/how-long-should-a-resume-be))
- "1 – 2 pages in length" for industry resumes; CVs are "As many pages as needed". ([GT Career Center Guide, pp. 10–12](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf))
- "In one to two pages … you give a strong account of yourself"; "In Canada, most employers expect
  to see a resumé that is one to two pages long"; "Most resumés are initially scanned for about
  10 seconds." ([University of Toronto Toolkit](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf))
- Academic CVs: "a maximum of two to three pages" for postdoc/fellowship applications,
  "slightly more" for lectureships, and "your CV should focus on the last five years." ([Imperial College](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/staff-development/public/postdocs/tipsheets/Academic-CVs-v2019.pdf))
- Australia (market convention): "your resume should be no longer than two to three pages. If you
  are a student, graduate, or early-career professional, one to two pages may be enough." ([Robert Half AU — How to write a resume](https://www.roberthalf.com/au/en/insights/landing-job/resume))

Convergence: **1 page up to ~10 years of experience; 2 pages when the relevant evidence
requires it; academic CVs exempt.** Scan time is seconds — "Recruiters spend just a few
seconds on average" ([MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resumes/)); the UofT toolkit
says about 10 seconds ([UofT](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf)),
Imperial assumes "a quick scan … maximum 30 seconds" ([Imperial College](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/staff-development/public/postdocs/tipsheets/Academic-CVs-v2019.pdf)).

### 1.8 Entry and content conventions everyone repeats

- Reverse chronological order, consistent date placement: "all the dates in the same place" ([UofT Toolkit](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf));
  "List items in reverse chronological order" ([GT 1000](https://sites.gatech.edu/gt1000-textbook/career-development/resumes-linkedin-and-more/resume-appearance-and-organization/));
  "Stick to a chronological resume format, not a functional one" ([Workable](https://resources.workable.com/stories-and-insights/how-ATS-reads-resumes)).
- Dates: "Month and Year for timeframe employed … Spell the month out" ([GT Career Center Guide, p. 4](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf));
  Stanford's samples use "Dates, including month and year" ([Stanford Career Education — Resume & Cover Letter Examples](https://careered.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22801/files/media/file/resume-and-cover-letter-examples.pdf)).
- Bullets, not sentences: "Include bullets; no full sentences" ([GT Career Center Guide, p. 12](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf));
  "Never use full sentences anywhere on your CV" and "use concise, punchy bullet points" ([Imperial College](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/staff-development/public/postdocs/tipsheets/Academic-CVs-v2019.pdf)).
- No photos: Harvard lists "Include a picture" under DON'T ([Harvard FAS MCS](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/));
  GT lists "Include a photo of yourself on your resume" under DON'T ([GT Career Center Guide, p. 10](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf));
  Canada excludes photos by law ([UofT Toolkit](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf));
  photos are a named parse failure ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)).
- No references list: "List references" is a Harvard DON'T ([Harvard FAS MCS](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/));
  "Unless specifically requested, do not include or refer to references" ([UofT Toolkit](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf))
  — but note Australian convention includes referees ([University of Melbourne — Resumes](https://students.unimelb.edu.au/careers/get-career-ready/resumes)).
- Don't lead lines with dates: "Start each line with a date" is a Harvard DON'T ([Harvard FAS MCS](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/)).

### 1.9 Typography details for labels and headings

- All-caps and small-caps text needs positive tracking: "you always add 5–12% extra
  letterspacing to text in all caps or small caps. This is particularly important at small
  sizes" — `letter-spacing: 0.05em` to `0.12em`. Body text is designed for "approximately
  9–13 point"; below 9 pt lowercase may also need slight tracking. ([Butterick — Letterspacing](https://practicaltypography.com/letterspacing.html))
- But the ATS limit applies: literal spaces between letters are a documented parse failure
  ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse))
  and "Don't fiddle with character spacing" is Workable's rule ([Workable](https://resources.workable.com/stories-and-insights/how-ATS-reads-resumes)).
  Resolution in §2.5.
- Text alignment: "align all text to the left (never use centre or justified)". ([Imperial College](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/staff-development/public/postdocs/tipsheets/Academic-CVs-v2019.pdf))

## 2. Where the sources disagree, and how to resolve it for a product

### 2.1 Exact body size: 10 vs 11 vs 12 pt

MIT allows 10 pt ([MIT CAPD Toolkit](https://cdn.uconnectlabs.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/123/2025/01/MIT-CAPDs-Career-Toolkit-Crafting-an-effective-resume.pdf));
GT says 11–12 ([GT Career Center Guide](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf));
Imperial says 11+ ([Imperial College](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/staff-development/public/postdocs/tipsheets/Academic-CVs-v2019.pdf));
Butterick's print optimum is 10–12 ([Butterick](https://practicaltypography.com/point-size.html)).
**Resolution:** ship a default of 10.5 pt with a supported band of 10–12 pt; treat 10 pt as
"compact" only, 11 pt as the "classic/academic" default. Because apparent size varies by
family ([Butterick](https://practicaltypography.com/point-size.html)), let `fontScale` nudge
within this band rather than allowing sub-10 pt output.

### 2.2 Serif vs sans

Imperial prefers sans ([Imperial College](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/staff-development/public/postdocs/tipsheets/Academic-CVs-v2019.pdf));
MIT accepts "Times New Roman, Arial" ([MIT CAPD Toolkit](https://cdn.uconnectlabs.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/123/2025/01/MIT-CAPDs-Career-Toolkit-Crafting-an-effective-resume.pdf));
GT 1000 lists "Calibri, Arial, Times New Roman" ([GT 1000](https://sites.gatech.edu/gt1000-textbook/career-development/resumes-linkedin-and-more/resume-appearance-and-organization/));
Robert Half adds Aptos ([Robert Half AU](https://www.roberthalf.com/au/en/insights/landing-job/resume));
consulting expects "standard serif font like Times New Roman" ([Hacking the Case Interview — Consulting Application Mistakes](https://www.hackingthecaseinterview.com/pages/consulting-application-mistakes)).
**Resolution:** both kinds are safe if the face is a text face; offer serif and sans presets
built from the accepted families only (Times New Roman/Tinos, Arial/Arimo, Helvetica/Arimo,
Calibri/Carlito, Georgia). Never a display or script face. One family per document is the
parser-safe rule ([Workable](https://resources.workable.com/stories-and-insights/how-ATS-reads-resumes)).

### 2.3 Margins: 0.5 in vs 1 in

MIT's floor is 0.5 in ([MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resumes/)),
Imperial's norm is 1 in ([Imperial College](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/staff-development/public/postdocs/tipsheets/Academic-CVs-v2019.pdf)).
**Resolution:** default to ~0.6–0.75 in (43–54 pt) sides and ≥0.5 in top/bottom; 0.5 in is
the compact floor, 1 in is the academic/classic preset. Never below 0.5 in.

### 2.4 Page count: US one-page habit vs UK/AU two-to-three pages

US guidance: one page to ~10 years ([Indeed](https://au.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/how-long-should-a-resume-be),
[MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resumes/)); UK academic: 2–3 pages ([Imperial College](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/staff-development/public/postdocs/tipsheets/Academic-CVs-v2019.pdf));
Australia: 2–3 pages, 1–2 for early career ([Robert Half AU](https://www.roberthalf.com/au/en/insights/landing-job/resume));
Canada: 1–2 pages ([UofT Toolkit](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf)).
**Resolution:** don't hard-limit page count; provide density presets and warn (softly) when a
"Compact one-page" run-over happens. Page count is a market convention, not a parse issue —
GT itself says academic CVs take "As many pages as needed" ([GT Career Center Guide](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf)).

### 2.5 Tracked (letter-spaced) headings: typography says yes, parsers say no

Butterick requires 5–12% tracking on caps/small-caps labels ([Butterick — Letterspacing](https://practicaltypography.com/letterspacing.html));
Greenhouse lists "a resume with spaces between the letters" as a parse failure ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse))
and Workable says "Don't fiddle with character spacing" ([Workable](https://resources.workable.com/stories-and-insights/how-ATS-reads-resumes)).
**Resolution:** the Greenhouse failure is *literal space characters* between letters
("E X P E R I E N C E") and extreme tracking that extraction turns into spaces. Use
font-level tracking (`letter-spacing`) at the low end (≤ 0.08em ≈ 8%), never insert literal
spaces, and never exceed the family's natural spacing at display sizes. The parser-safe
default is small-caps or uppercase + weight, with a subtle tracking bump.

### 2.6 Header/footer page numbers: useful for CVs, fatal for ATS contact blocks

GT *recommends* "Header or footer with your name and page number" for academic CVs
([GT Career Center Guide](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf));
Greenhouse names headers/footers (and contact info inside them) as parse failures
([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse));
Workable warns header/footer text "get[s] interspersed with the main body of text"
([Workable](https://resources.workable.com/stories-and-insights/how-ATS-reads-resumes)).
**Resolution:** contact details always in the body top. Page numbers only as an optional,
documented risk in the Academic CV archetype (page numbers are low-stakes if garbled;
contact details are not).

### 2.7 Dates: "spell the month out" vs "dates in full (day, month, year)" vs MM/YYYY

GT: "Spell the month out" ([GT Career Center Guide](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf));
Workable: "include dates in full (day, month and year)" for parsers ([Workable](https://resources.workable.com/stories-and-insights/how-ATS-reads-resumes));
UK convention is typically MM/YYYY; Workday notes parsing varies by format and word order
([Olive](https://olive.is/answers/resume-file-format-parser-breaks/)).
**Resolution:** one consistent `dateFormat` per resume (already a `ResumeSettings` field),
defaulting to locale-appropriate "Jan 2024 – Mar 2025" (or "Jan. 2024 – Mar. 2025"); day-level
dates are unnecessary for employment ranges. Consistency matters more than the specific
pattern ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)).

### 2.8 Colour: accepted in some creative markets, advised against almost everywhere else

Consulting expects "black text, white background" ([Hacking the Case Interview](https://www.hackingthecaseinterview.com/pages/consulting-application-mistakes));
Robert Half warns against "complex graphics, excessive design elements, or layouts that
distract" and says overdesigned resumes "risk being overlooked early in the process"
([Robert Half AU](https://www.roberthalf.com/au/en/insights/landing-job/resume));
Workable: "soft pastel font colours, fancy fonts and WordArt headings … will just look like
noise to a parser" ([Workable](https://resources.workable.com/stories-and-insights/how-ATS-reads-resumes));
GT: too many styling elements deter a careful read ([GT 1000](https://sites.gatech.edu/gt1000-textbook/career-development/resumes-linkedin-and-more/resume-appearance-and-organization/)).
**Resolution:** colour is confined to one accent (rules/labels/name), body text is never
coloured, backgrounds are never filled. Creative tolerance is a niche case; the default must
be printable in greyscale without loss.

## 3. Recommended canonical typographic scale

Values are for `fontScale = 1.0`, expressed in **pt** (see §1 for evidence). The
"supported band" is what the product allows; the default is the canonical value.

| Role | Default | Supported band | Weight | Line height | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Name | 18 pt | 16–20 pt | 600–700 | 1.05 | GT 1000: "typically 16-18 pt works well" ([GT 1000](https://sites.gatech.edu/gt1000-textbook/career-development/resumes-linkedin-and-more/resume-appearance-and-organization/)); name is "the largest font … but should not take up too much space" ([GT Career Center Guide, p. 3](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf)). Cap at ~2× body — no size inflation ([Butterick](https://practicaltypography.com/point-size.html)). |
| Headline / target title | 11 pt | 10.5–12 pt | 400 (italic or regular, secondary ink) | 1.1 | A role line under the name, not a display headline. |
| Section heading | 11 pt | 10.5–12 pt | 600 | 1.1 | UPPERCASE + ≤0.08em tracking, or Title Case bold. See §5. |
| Entry title (role/degree) | 10.5 pt | 10–11.5 pt | 600 | 1.15 | Bold "works well for … emphasising job titles" ([Imperial College](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/staff-development/public/postdocs/tipsheets/Academic-CVs-v2019.pdf)). |
| Meta line (org, location, dates) | 9.5 pt | 9–10.5 pt | 400 | 1.1 | Same line as entry title or directly below; dates in one consistent column/placement ([UofT Toolkit](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf)). Floor 9 pt ([Butterick](https://practicaltypography.com/letterspacing.html)). |
| Body / bullets | 10.5 pt | 10–12 pt | 400 | 1.25 compact / 1.35 normal | Band from §1.1; line-height band 1.2–1.45 ([Butterick](https://practicaltypography.com/line-spacing.html)). |
| Small labels (skills, badges) | 9.5 pt | 9–10.5 pt | 400–600 | 1.15 | If uppercased, tracking per §2.5. |

**Weight policy:** 400 / 600 / 700 only. Italic used at most once per entry (e.g. thesis
title). Underlining reserved for links at most — "capitals, italics, and underlining can
clutter the page and are often best avoided" ([Imperial College](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/staff-development/public/postdocs/tipsheets/Academic-CVs-v2019.pdf)).

**Safe font families and metric-compatible fallbacks** (one family per document
([Workable](https://resources.workable.com/stories-and-insights/how-ATS-reads-resumes));
the accepted set across [MIT](https://cdn.uconnectlabs.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/123/2025/01/MIT-CAPDs-Career-Toolkit-Crafting-an-effective-resume.pdf),
[GT 1000](https://sites.gatech.edu/gt1000-textbook/career-development/resumes-linkedin-and-more/resume-appearance-and-organization/),
[Imperial](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/staff-development/public/postdocs/tipsheets/Academic-CVs-v2019.pdf),
[Robert Half](https://www.roberthalf.com/au/en/insights/landing-job/resume)):

| Family | Kind | Metric-compatible fallback | Permitted in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Times New Roman | serif | Tinos | all presets |
| Arial | sans | Arimo | all presets |
| Helvetica | sans | Arimo | all presets |
| Calibri | sans | Carlito | modern/standard presets |
| Georgia | serif | Gelasio | standard/academic |
| Lato, Source Sans, Montserrat (already vendored in `public/fonts/`) | sans | — | opt-in, industry-neutral; verify weights render (see repo font pipeline notes in `docs/AGENT-CONTEXT.md` §2.13) |

Substitutions must preserve generic kind and metrics (Tinos↔Times, Arimo↔Arial/Helvetica,
Carlito↔Calibri) because a change in advance widths reflows line breaks and silently changes
page count — a rule this repo's font pipeline already enforces
(`docs/AGENT-CONTEXT.md` §2.13).

**Repo gap this scale fixes** (observation only; no code changed): `sections.typ` stores the
type scale in px and converts with `px-to-pt` (×0.75), so default `body: 11` renders at
**8.25 pt** in entry content and `small: 10` at 7.5 pt — below every floor in §1.1 — while
`minimal.typ`'s header renders the same values as pt (`* 1pt`, name 24 pt). Margins default
to 36 (px) → 27 pt = **0.375 in**, below MIT's 0.5 in floor
([MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resumes/)). The canonical scale above should be
defined in pt directly, in one place.

## 4. Recommended spacing system

A small modular scale in pt (justified by §1.4 whitespace norms and the density goal; the
"adjust line spacing first" rule is the primary density control
([Butterick](https://practicaltypography.com/line-spacing.html))):

| Step | pt | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| xs | 2 | typographic fine-tuning: name→headline, tracking-adjacent gaps |
| s | 3 | between bullets in one entry (tight cluster) ([GT 1000](https://sites.gatech.edu/gt1000-textbook/career-development/resumes-linkedin-and-more/resume-appearance-and-organization/): "Maintain uniformity of length and construction of bullets") |
| m | 4 | section heading → first entry; meta line → first bullet |
| l | 6 | between entries within a section |
| xl | 8 | compact mode: gap above section heading |
| 2xl | 12 | normal mode: gap above section heading / between sections ([Harvard FAS MCS](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/): "consistent spacing"; [GT 1000](https://sites.gatech.edu/gt1000-textbook/career-development/resumes-linkedin-and-more/resume-appearance-and-organization/): "Separate sections with white space") |
| 3xl | 16 | header block → first section; spacious mode section gaps (20 pt cap) |

- `sectionSpacing` presets map cleanly: compact = 8 pt above headings / 3 pt bullets,
  normal = 12 / 3, spacious = 16–20 / 4. (Current `sections.typ` 8/12/20 values are within
  this system; keep them.)
- **Margins:** default 0.6–0.75 in (43–54 pt) left/right, 0.5–0.6 in (36–43 pt) top/bottom;
  compact floor 0.5 in / 36 pt ([MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resumes/));
  classic preset 1 in / 72 pt ([Imperial College](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/staff-development/public/postdocs/tipsheets/Academic-CVs-v2019.pdf)).
- **Bullets:** hanging indent ≈ 1.2× the bullet glyph width (≈ 10–12 pt indent at 10.5 pt
  body); bullet glyph is a simple round bullet or en-dash — one style throughout
  ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse):
  "differing formats throughout each section" is a failure cause).
- **Density budget:** at 10.5 pt / 1.25 leading on Letter with 0.6 in margins, a page holds
  roughly 45–50 text lines — about 6–7 entries with 2–3 bullets each. That budget, not
  decoration, is what the spacing scale manages.

## 5. Section heading conventions

**Wording parsers and recruiters recognise** — use conventional nouns as heading text
([Workable](https://resources.workable.com/stories-and-insights/how-ATS-reads-resumes):
"Use typical names for section titles like 'Education', 'Work Experience', 'Personal Details'";
[Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse):
"Resumes without clear sections" fail):

`Experience` / `Work Experience` · `Education` · `Skills` · `Projects` · `Leadership` /
`Volunteer Experience` · `Certifications` · `Awards` / `Honors` · `Publications` ·
`Languages` · `Summary` / `Professional Summary` · `References` (AU only)

Stanford's samples demonstrate split variants that stay conventional: "Research Experience",
"Teaching Experience", "Leadership Experience" ([Stanford Career Education](https://careered.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22801/files/media/file/resume-and-cover-letter-examples.pdf));
GT endorses splitting by relevance ("Relevant Experience, Healthcare Experience, Data Analysis
Experience") ([GT Career Center Guide, p. 4](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf)).
Avoid coined headings ("Where I've Made an Impact") — Workable's job-title rule applies to
sections too: standards over wit ([Workable](https://resources.workable.com/stories-and-insights/how-ATS-reads-resumes)).

**Safe visual treatment** (in priority order):

1. **Weight + case:** 600 weight at 11 pt, either Title Case or UPPERCASE — the most
   universally endorsed device ([MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resumes/): bold
   "for … section headings"; [GT Career Center Guide](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf):
   "Use bold, ALL CAPS, italics & lines to facilitate scanning").
2. **Tracking:** +0.05–0.08em *font-level* tracking on UPPERCASE labels
   ([Butterick](https://practicaltypography.com/letterspacing.html)), never literal spaces
   ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)),
   never above 0.12em ([Workable](https://resources.workable.com/stories-and-insights/how-ATS-reads-resumes)).
3. **Optional hairline rule** (0.5 pt, text ink or a single restrained accent) below the
   heading — "lines to facilitate scanning" is endorsed by
   [GT Career Center Guide](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf);
   keep to one rule style per document ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)).
4. **Never:** small-caps faked with spaced capitals; heading background fills; heading
   icons/word art (parse failure: "graphics, photos, or word art",
   [Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)).

## 6. What to delete, explicitly

| Remove | Why the sources say so |
|---|---|
| Coloured body/heading text (tacky colours, secondary "grey-blue" body) | "soft pastel font colours … look like noise to a parser" ([Workable](https://resources.workable.com/stories-and-insights/how-ATS-reads-resumes)); conservative markets expect "black text, white background" ([Hacking the Case Interview](https://www.hackingthecaseinterview.com/pages/consulting-application-mistakes)); must survive greyscale printing. |
| Section background fills / shaded header bars | Fills render as graphics in extraction and are listed among parse-breaking "graphics" ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)); "too many styling elements" deter careful reading ([GT 1000](https://sites.gatech.edu/gt1000-textbook/career-development/resumes-linkedin-and-more/resume-appearance-and-organization/)). |
| Icons in headings or as date/role markers | "graphics, photos, or word art" are a named parse failure; a dated icon is "information that is visually obvious to you and invisible to whatever reads the file" ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse), [Olive](https://olive.is/answers/resume-file-format-parser-breaks/)). |
| Photos | Harvard DON'T ([Harvard FAS MCS](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/)); GT DON'T ([GT Career Center Guide](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf)); illegal to request in Canada ([UofT Toolkit](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf)); parse failure ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)). |
| Decorative rules / ornamental dividers beyond one hairline style | Consistency is a parse requirement — "differing formats throughout each section" ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)); decoration over content hurts scannability ([GT 1000](https://sites.gatech.edu/gt1000-textbook/career-development/resumes-linkedin-and-more/resume-appearance-and-organization/)). |
| Multi-column / table layouts | "Don't use tables and columns as the ordering of sentences may not be what you expect" ([Workable](https://resources.workable.com/stories-and-insights/how-ATS-reads-resumes)); "Resumes that have a columned layout" fail to parse ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)); "avoid tables that limit your content" ([GT Career Center Guide](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf)); "Do not use columns" ([Cornell CALS standard resume template](https://career.cornell.edu/resources/cals-standard-resume-template/)). |
| Text boxes (incl. contact details in header/footer/text box) | Named parse failure: "Resumes with the name and contact information in the header, footer, or text box" ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)); "avoid using headers and footers" ([Workable](https://resources.workable.com/stories-and-insights/how-ATS-reads-resumes)). |
| WordArt / letter-spaced display headings with literal spaces | Named failures: "word art", "a resume with spaces between the letters" ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)); "Don't use WordArt", "Don't fiddle with character spacing" ([Workable](https://resources.workable.com/stories-and-insights/how-ATS-reads-resumes)). |
| Justified / centred body text | "align all text to the left (never use centre or justified)" ([Imperial College](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/staff-development/public/postdocs/tipsheets/Academic-CVs-v2019.pdf)). |
| Skill bars, rating dots, QR codes, logos | Stand-ins for written text; "information … invisible to whatever reads the file" ([Olive](https://olive.is/answers/resume-file-format-parser-breaks/)); "graphics" ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)). |

Keep exactly two decorative affordances: one accent colour (rules/labels only) and one
optional hairline rule per section heading.

## 7. Template archetypes to build (7)

All share: single column, left-aligned, standard section vocabulary (§5), canonical scale (§3)
scaled by `fontScale`, spacing scale (§4). They differ by **structure and density**, not colour.

### 7.1 Compact one-page
- **For:** students, new graduates, ≤ ~10 years ([Indeed](https://au.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/how-long-should-a-resume-be), [MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resumes/)).
- **Layout:** name + contact line (body text, top of body), optional one-line summary, Education + Experience + Skills (+ Projects). No headline.
- **Type/spacing:** body 10 pt / 1.2; name 16 pt; meta 9 pt; margins 0.5 in; section gap 8 pt; entries 5–6 pt; bullets 2–3 per entry.

### 7.2 Standard professional
- **For:** mid-career industry applicants across sectors.
- **Layout:** name + headline + contact; `Summary` (3 lines max — Robert Half places "personal summary" in the top half ([Robert Half AU](https://www.roberthalf.com/au/en/insights/landing-job/resume))); Experience first, then Education, Skills.
- **Type/spacing:** body 10.5 pt / 1.25; name 18 pt; margins 0.6–0.75 in; section gap 12 pt; entries 6 pt. Target 1 page, spill allowed to 2.

### 7.3 Technical / engineering
- **For:** software, data, engineering roles ([MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resumes/): "if you wrote software, say what language you used"; [GT Career Center Guide](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf): skills + projects sections).
- **Layout:** Summary optional; `Skills` as category: items lines (e.g. "Languages: Python, Go"); `Projects` before/after Experience; no skill bars or icons (§6).
- **Type/spacing:** body 10 pt / 1.22; name 16–18 pt; margins 0.5–0.6 in; category labels 600 at 9.5 pt; section gap 10 pt.

### 7.4 Graduate / entry (education-first)
- **For:** current students and fresh graduates ([GT 1000](https://sites.gatech.edu/gt1000-textbook/career-development/resumes-linkedin-and-more/resume-appearance-and-organization/): education first until after graduation; [Stanford Career Education](https://careered.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22801/files/media/file/resume-and-cover-letter-examples.pdf) samples).
- **Layout:** Education (degree, dates, GPA scale, coursework lines) → Experience/Internships → Leadership → Skills.
- **Type/spacing:** body 10.5 pt / 1.25; name 18 pt; margins 0.6 in; one page hard target; dates right-aligned column-free (trailing or fixed right edge in body flow).

### 7.5 Academic CV
- **For:** postdoc, fellowship, faculty applications ([Imperial College](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/staff-development/public/postdocs/tipsheets/Academic-CVs-v2019.pdf), [GT Career Center Guide](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf)).
- **Layout:** Name + contact in body; `Education`, `Employment`, `Publications` (appendix-style, numbered), `Grants & Awards`, `Teaching`, `Service`; references at end optional; page numbers in footer **optional and flagged as ATS-risk** (§2.6).
- **Type/spacing:** body 11 pt / 1.3; name 18–20 pt; margins 1 in; section gap 16 pt; 2–3+ pages, no hard cap ("As many pages as needed").

### 7.6 Executive summary-first
- **For:** directors, VPs, C-level; long histories condensed ([Indeed](https://au.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/how-long-should-a-resume-be): executives tend to have lengthier resumes; content over length).
- **Layout:** Name + headline; `Executive Summary` (4–5 lines, or 3 bullets with metrics); `Core Expertise` as one comma-separated line (not a table); selected roles with 4–6 bullets each; earlier career compressed to title/company/dates lines.
- **Type/spacing:** body 10.5–11 pt / 1.3; name 20 pt; margins 0.75 in; section gap 14–16 pt; 2 pages.

### 7.7 UK / Commonwealth CV
- **For:** UK, Canada, Australia, NZ applications (A4 paper; "CV" naming) ([UofT Toolkit](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/CC-Resume-and-Cover-Letter-Toolkit.pdf): 1–2 pages Canada; [Robert Half AU](https://www.roberthalf.com/au/en/insights/landing-job/resume): 2–3 pages AU; [University of Melbourne](https://students.unimelb.edu.au/careers/get-career-ready/resumes): referees section).
- **Layout:** A4 page size; `Profile` first (UK convention); `Education`/`Professional Experience`; `Skills`; `Referees` section at end (or "Available on request").
- **Type/spacing:** body 10.5 pt / 1.25; name 18 pt; margins 0.6–0.75 in; date format `MM/YYYY` or "Jan 2024 – Mar 2025" via `dateFormat`; 2 pages typical.

Market note for the product: paper size is part of the archetype — Letter for US presets,
A4 for UK/Commonwealth presets (submitting A4 to a US employer distorts printing
([CV2Go — Resume Formatting](https://cv2go.no/guide-to-resume-formatting-layout/))).

## 8. Open questions for the maintainer

1. **Units and migration for `ResumeSettings`.** The scale is currently stored in px and
   converted `×0.75` (`sections.typ:61`), producing body text at 8.25 pt and margins at
   0.375 in — both below every published floor (§1.1, §1.2). Do we redefine the stored scale
   in pt (breaking existing saved metadata), or keep px and change the conversion (silent
   reflow of every existing resume)? Either way, `minimal.typ` (which treats values as pt)
   and `sections.typ` (which converts) must be reconciled. Note the `resume.update` metadata
   merge behaviour (`docs/AGENT-CONTEXT.md` §2.17) affects any migration.

2. **Page size per market.** Letter-only today. Do we add an A4 page-size setting (needed for
   archetype 7 and every non-US user), and should it be a per-template default, a
   `ResumeSettings` field, or both?

3. **Footer page numbers in the Academic archetype.** GT recommends them
   ([GT Career Center Guide](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/c/3073/files/2024/02/Career_Center_Resume_Guide-645d43267633e4df.pdf)),
   Greenhouse documents headers/footers as parse risk
   ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)).
   Ship the option with a warning, or forbid footers everywhere and set academic page numbers
   in the body flow (bottom margin area) instead?

4. **Heading tracking cap.** Butterick wants 5–12% tracking on caps
   ([Butterick](https://practicaltypography.com/letterspacing.html)); Workable says don't
   fiddle with character spacing ([Workable](https://resources.workable.com/stories-and-insights/how-ATS-reads-resumes)).
   Confirm the cap (proposed: font-level `letter-spacing` ≤ 0.08em, literal spaces never) and
   decide whether `accentStyle: "underline"` should be retired or kept as a non-default relic
   (underlining is discouraged by [Imperial College](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/staff-development/public/postdocs/tipsheets/Academic-CVs-v2019.pdf)).

5. **Per-market conventions baked into archetypes, or user-configurable?** Referees
   (AU: [University of Melbourne](https://students.unimelb.edu.au/careers/get-career-ready/resumes))
   vs no references (US: [Harvard FAS MCS](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/)),
   date formats (§2.7), "CV" vs "Resume" naming, one-page vs two-page expectations (§2.4).
   Do archetypes lock these (simpler, opinionated), or expose them as settings (more surface,
   more ways to produce a bad document)?
